# 07 — Accessibility (a11y)

> 🚧 **Status:** Syllabus ready — 8 lessons outlined, full content in progress.

Accessibility isn't a checklist bolted on at the end — it's mostly a byproduct of writing correct semantic HTML in the first place. This module covers the remaining 20%: focus management, ARIA, testing, and legal context.

## Lessons

| # | Lesson | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | [Intro to Web Accessibility](./01-intro-to-web-accessibility.md) | What web accessibility means in practice, and why it's a core engineering skill, not an afterthought. |
| 02 | [Accessible Names and Descriptions](./02-accessible-names-and-descriptions.md) | How assistive technology computes an element's accessible name and description. |
| 03 | [Keyboard Navigation and Focus Management](./03-keyboard-navigation-and-focus-management.md) | Tab order, visible focus states, and skip links for keyboard-only users. |
| 04 | [Aria Attributes in HTML](./04-aria-attributes-in-html.md) | Common ARIA attributes and the golden rule: no ARIA is better than bad ARIA. |
| 05 | [Forms Accessibility Labels Errors](./05-forms-accessibility-labels-errors.md) | Accessible form labeling and error messaging patterns. |
| 06 | [Tables and Complex Content](./06-tables-and-complex-content.md) | Making data tables and complex interactive widgets accessible. |
| 07 | [Testing with Screen Readers](./07-testing-with-screen-readers.md) | Manually testing pages with VoiceOver and NVDA. |
| 08 | [Wcag and Legal Requirements](./08-wcag-and-legal-requirements.md) | WCAG conformance levels and the legal landscape (ADA, EN 301 549) around accessibility. |
